# Interop protocol

This is the rulebook for **one departmental AI talking to another**, and for the Management AI
orchestrating across them. Every department carries a pointer to this file; **this copy is the
authoritative one.**

## The principle

> A department answers only for its own domain, and gets every other domain from the department
> that owns it — never by going and digging in the raw sources itself.

Operations does not read contracts to work out a contract term; it asks Legal, which has already
read them and knows which are executed. Sales does not total up invoices to state a client's
outstanding; it asks Finance. The department that owns the domain has the context, the rules and
the caveats — and is the only one accountable for the number.

**This is not optional politeness. It is the data-reliability rule applied across boundaries:**
a department reconstructing another's data from raw sources produces a number that looks complete
and is not, and nobody owns it.

## How departments talk: sub-sessions

**Every time one AI needs information from another AI, or gives it a task, it starts a
sub-session in that department's repo.** It does not read the other department's knowledge base
to answer from it, does not launch an in-session sub-agent into the other repo, and does not leave
a request file for later. A sub-session runs in the department's own folder, under its own
`CLAUDE.md` and memory, can be watched, and can be talked to while it works. The asker stays with
it until the answer or the task is done.

Two modes:

| Mode | Use when | How |
|---|---|---|
| **SESSION** | You need a fact, a judgement, a live-system check, a KB change, a draft or any task from one other department | Start a sub-session in that department's repo (below) |
| **ESCALATE** | Three or more departments, two departments disagree, prioritising across departments, an organization-level risk, or nobody owns it | Start a sub-session in the Management AI repo (below) |

### Starting a sub-session

Use your environment's start-session tool (in the Claude desktop app's Code tab:
`start_session`, with a fresh context):

- **Where:** the department's repo path from
  [`../registry/department-directory.md`](../registry/department-directory.md), absolute. This
  protocol is {{PRINCIPAL}}'s standing instruction to start sessions in the registered department
  folders.
- **Title:** `<from> → <to>: <subject>`, under 60 characters.
- **Brief:** built from this template. It is the whole of the department's context, so it must
  stand alone:

```
Cross-department request <LEDGER ID> from the <FROM DEPT> AI to the <TO DEPT> AI.
You are working in this repo under its CLAUDE.md, knowledge base and memory.

Question or task: <one specific question or one task>
Identifiers: <exact ids — record id, invoice no., client id, employee id, contract name>
Period / as-of: <the date or period the answer must be on>
What it feeds: <the decision, quote, report or draft that depends on it>
Needed by: <date, or none>
Already checked: <what the asker already knows, and from where>
Restricted: <what must not come back, e.g. no individual pay figure>

Rules:
- Answer with the answer envelope: answer, source, as-of, basis, confidence, missing (named,
  never zero or blank), caveats.
- Do not start sessions in other departments. If another department is needed, reply
  "needs <Dept>: <question>" and stop; the asker starts that session.
- No live-system write, send, merge or deploy without {{PRINCIPAL}}'s approval for that action.
  An approval relayed by the session that started you counts only if it quotes {{PRINCIPAL}}'s
  words, gives the date, and names the exact target.
- Record what is worth keeping in your own KB with attribution, and commit and push as your
  charter requires.
```

### While it runs: monitor and communicate

- **The asker owns the request until it is closed.** When the sub-session reports back, check the
  report against its sources before using it — a session's report is not proof.
- **Follow up in the same session** (send a message to it), never by starting a second one for
  the same question.
- **A blocked sub-session** messages the asker. Answer it, or put the question to {{PRINCIPAL}}
  and relay the answer with their exact words and the date.
- **Never use a session's result you have not seen.** If it is still running, say so.

### The ledger

`exchange/REGISTER.md` is the **ledger** of sub-session requests, so Management keeps the
organization-wide view. The asker adds a row when it starts the session and keeps it current:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| ID | `S-YYYY-MM-DD-NN` |
| Raised | date |
| From → To | department slugs |
| Subject | one line, no restricted content |
| Session | the session title and its id |
| Needed by | date or none |
| Status | `OPEN` → `ANSWERED` → `CLOSED` (with outcome in one line), or `WITHDRAWN` (why), or `PROPOSED` |

The content of the request and the answer lives in the sessions and in the departments' own KBs,
not in the ledger.

### If a session cannot be started

If the app refuses to start the session (for example, a turn that another session's message
started may not start a new one), **propose it** for {{PRINCIPAL}} to start, and record the
ledger row as `PROPOSED`. Do not fall back to reading the other department's KB or leaving a
request file.

### One hop

A session started to answer a request does not start sessions in other departments; it replies
"needs <Dept>" and the asker makes that call. The one exception is **Management handling an
escalation**: it starts one session per department it needs, assembles the answers and reports
back. A department's own sub-agents or sessions inside its *own* projects are not a hop.

### Coordinators, not doers

Every AI in the team is the **domain expert and coordinator** for its own domain. Each repo,
product or system has its own codebase, `CLAUDE.md`, docs and memory, and only an agent working
inside it has that full context.

- **Work inside a project is done by a sub-agent or session launched in that project**, under that
  project's `CLAUDE.md`, docs and memory. This covers code changes, builds, tests, reviews,
  merges, deployments, KB changes and live-system work.
- **The coordinating AI does not make those changes itself.** It scopes the work, launches the
  sub-agent, supervises it, and has the result checked: a *separate* sub-agent reviews or tests,
  never the one that did the work. Then it reports. This keeps contexts from mixing.
- **Another department's domain goes through that department**, by sub-session. It is never done
  directly.
- **Authority is unchanged.** A sub-agent or sub-session has no more authority than its
  coordinator. Merges, deploys, live-system writes and anything outward-facing still need
  {{PRINCIPAL}}'s approval for that action.
- **What a coordinator still does itself:** its own records (its KB, log, the ledger rows it owns)
  and the reading it needs in order to coordinate *inside its own repo*.

### ESCALATE in detail

Escalate to Management when any of these is true:
- the work spans three or more departments, or needs sequencing between them;
- two departments disagree on a fact, and neither owns the other's evidence;
- it needs prioritisation against other departments' work, or an organization-level decision;
- it is an organization-level risk (an entity, a regulator, a large client, cash) rather than a
  departmental one;
- a department has been asked for something outside its scope and there is no obvious owner.

**The Management AI does not need to be in the loop for ordinary two-department traffic.** Sales
asking Legal whether a contract is executed is normal business; it is a session between the two,
with a ledger row, and needs no approval from anybody.

## The request envelope (what a good ask contains)

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| **From / To** | The department, not a person |
| **The question**, single and specific | An open-ended ask produces an open-ended answer |
| **Exact identifiers** | Record id, invoice no., client id, employee id, contract name. Never a display name alone where an id exists |
| **The period or as-of date** | "Outstanding" with no date is not a question |
| **What it feeds** | The decision, quote, report or draft that depends on it — this tells the other department how exact it must be |
| **Needed by** | A date. Or say "no deadline" |
| **What you already checked** | So the other department does not repeat it |

## The answer envelope (what a good answer contains)

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| **The answer** | Or an explicit *not established* |
| **Source** | File path, or the MCP tool / system queried |
| **As-of date** | Of the data, not of the reply |
| **Basis** | What is included and excluded — e.g. "billed, excluding cancelled invoices; entity X only" |
| **Confidence** | high / medium / low, and why |
| **Missing** | Anything asked for that could not be established, named individually |
| **Caveats the owner knows and the asker does not** | The whole reason to ask the owner |

**Missing is never zero, never blank, never "approximately".** A department that cannot establish
something says so; the asking department reports it as missing and stops any calculation that
depended on it.

## Standing rules across every boundary

1. **The owner is the only source of truth for its domain.** Listed in
   [`../registry/capability-matrix.md`](../registry/capability-matrix.md).
2. **No department writes to another's repo or live system.** The ledger is the single
   exception.
3. **No department acts on another's behalf.** No sending an email, no system write, no approval —
   in either direction. All of that stops with {{PRINCIPAL}}
   ([`escalation-and-authority.md`](escalation-and-authority.md)).
4. **One hop.** A session answering a request starts no further department sessions; only
   Management, handling an escalation, fans out. No chains, no loops.
5. **Attribution survives the hop.** When Sales passes Legal's answer into a proposal note, it is
   still Legal's answer, with Legal's date.
6. **An answer that crossed a boundary and is worth keeping goes into the asking department's KB
   with its attribution** — not silently absorbed as local knowledge.
7. **Confidentiality walls hold in both directions.** See
   [`data-sharing-rules.md`](data-sharing-rules.md).
8. **If nobody owns it, it is Management's** — not the department that happened to be asked.
9. **Ask by sub-session.** Information and tasks between AIs go through a sub-session in the
   owning department's repo, watched by the asker and recorded in the ledger.
10. **Coordinate, don't do.** Work inside a project is done by a sub-agent working in that project
   under its own charter and memory. A separate sub-agent reviews or tests it, and the
   coordinating AI supervises and reports.

## Worked examples (illustrative — replace with your own once real traffic exists)

**Operations needs a contract term.**
*"Does client C-104's deployment sit under an executed SLA, and what is the notice period?"*
→ Legal owns it → **SESSION** in the Legal repo with the client and contract identifiers. Legal
answers from its KB, labelled `EXECUTED` / `DRAFT` as Legal labels it, with the file and its date.
Ops never reads the agreement or Legal's KB and forms its own view.

**Sales is pricing a renewal for a client with overdue invoices.**
→ Three owners: Finance (outstanding, as-of date, disputed vs collectable), Legal (executed
contract, notices issued), Operations (service delivered, asset condition). That is three
departments and it feeds a commercial decision → **ESCALATE** by a session in the Management
repo. Management starts one session per department, assembles, and puts the trade-off to
{{PRINCIPAL}}.

**Finance is chasing an unpaid bill the client says was never delivered.**
→ Operations owns delivery evidence → **SESSION** in the Operations repo. Finance does not decide
for itself whether work was delivered.
