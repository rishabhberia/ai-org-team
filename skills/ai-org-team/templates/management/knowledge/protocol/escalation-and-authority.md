# Escalation and authority

## Where authority actually sits

| Level | May decide |
|---|---|
| **{{PRINCIPAL}} (and the humans they name)** | Everything that changes a system, leaves the organization, or commits money: writes to any live business system; sending any email or message; submitting a bid; approving spend, pricing, an offer, a pay revision, a legal notice; and, at code level, merging or deploying a product, a store submission, a production migration, credential rotation, a DNS or hosting change — approved per action. Departments may draft and propose these |
| **Departmental AI** | What is true in its own domain, how to answer it, what its KB says, what it recommends |
| **Management AI** | Who answers a question; what order cross-department work happens in; what the organization-level open items and risks are; when something must go to {{PRINCIPAL}} |
| **Nobody else** | — |

The Management AI is **above the departments in routing, not in authority**. It cannot approve
what a department could not approve, and it cannot authorise a department to act. A sub-agent
delegated into a department inherits the department's authority, which for these purposes is none:
read, reason, report.

## What a department escalates

- Work that spans three or more departments, or needs sequencing between them.
- Two departments holding contradictory facts, where neither owns the other's evidence.
- A question with no owner in [`../registry/capability-matrix.md`](../registry/capability-matrix.md).
- Prioritisation: two departments wanting the same scarce thing (an asset, a person, cash, a date).
- An organization-level risk — an entity, a regulator, a major client, the cash position — as
  opposed to a departmental one.
- A department being asked to act outside its scope or against its own rules.

## What a department does **not** escalate

Ordinary two-department traffic. Sales asking Legal for a contract status, Finance asking
Operations for delivery evidence, a department asking HR whether someone still holds a role — these
are normal and need no management involvement, no approval and no log entry with Management. The
Management AI is a coordination point, not a checkpoint.

## What Management does with an escalation

1. **Establish the question.** One sentence, with the identifiers and the date. If it cannot be
   written that way, that is the first thing to fix.
2. **Name the owners** from the capability matrix, and what each must answer.
3. **Gather** — one sub-session per owning department, each with its own brief and ledger row.
   Follow up in those sessions; do not read the departments' KBs to answer from them.
4. **Assemble without blending.** Each department's answer keeps its source, date and caveats.
   Where they conflict, both positions are written down with their evidence; the conflict goes to
   [`../group/decision-register.md`](../group/decision-register.md) and to {{PRINCIPAL}}.
5. **State the decision needed** — what {{PRINCIPAL}} is actually being asked to decide, the
   options, and what is not known. Never decide it by default.
6. **Record it**: the decision register, the management log, and the ledger rows closed with the
   outcome.

## Conflicts between departments

When two departments disagree, the rule is *the owner of the domain owns the fact*, not *the more
recent file wins* and not *split the difference*:

- A contract term: **Legal**. A billed amount: **Finance**. Delivery: **Operations**. A quoted
  price: **Sales**. Employment status: **HR**. (Adjust to your departments in the capability
  matrix.)
- If the disagreement is *within* one owner's domain, that owner resolves it and says so.
- If it is genuinely cross-domain — the contract says one rate and the invoices show another —
  **that is a finding, not an error to be tidied away.** Record both, name it as a discrepancy,
  and escalate it. Someone is losing money in exactly these gaps.
