# Data sharing rules

Departments have separate knowledge bases partly for focus and partly because **some of what they
hold must not travel**. Interop makes travel easy, so the walls have to be explicit.

## How the walls hold under orchestration

1. **A request carries the question, not the record.** A session brief says *"what is
   the notice period on contract K-12?"* — it never pastes the contract. Restricted material stays
   where it is; the answer comes back as a conclusion.
2. **The narrowest answer that resolves the question.** A department asked whether someone may
   approve a repair answers *"yes / no, and here is the limit"* — not the person's pay, not their
   letter, not the approval matrix in full.
3. **A wall does not fall because Management asked.** The Management AI has no clearance beyond a
   department's own; it routes questions, it does not unlock records. A department refusing to pass
   restricted material to Management is behaving correctly, and says what it *can* answer instead.
4. **Session transcripts and the ledger persist.** A brief and its answer are kept in the session
   transcripts, and a ledger row is committed and pushed to the Management AI repo's history
   permanently. Write every brief, answer and row as if it will be read later by someone outside
   the conversation — because it will.
5. **Attribution travels with the fact.** When a department stores another's answer in its own KB,
   it records the source department, the file or system, and the as-of date. A stored answer is
   never upgraded into local knowledge.
6. **When in doubt, the owning department decides.** Not the asking one, and not Management.

## What this is not

This is not a reason to refuse ordinary cross-department work. The default for operational facts —
contract status, job state, implementation progress, an outstanding balance with its caveats, a
policy answer — is **share, with provenance**. The walls above are a short and specific list;
everything else flows.
